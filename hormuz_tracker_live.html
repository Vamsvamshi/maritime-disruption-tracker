Supply & Demand Signals

EIA global oil balance (July STEO, released Jul 7, data cut-off Jul 1): global liquid fuels production is forecast at 99.0 mb/d in 2026 before rising to 109.3 mb/d in 2027, as most Gulf shut-ins unwind; global consumption is forecast to fall by 1.2 mb/d in 2026 to roughly 102.9 mb/d before rebounding in 2027 — the first annual demand contraction of the crisis, driven almost entirely by Asia's exposure to Middle East barrels. That combination (supply recovering faster than demand) is what is pulling Brent down from a $95–105/bbl operating range in May-June to the $74–82/bbl range now baked into the forecast.EIA STEO, Global Oil Markets, Jul 2026¹⁶

Latest Developments

Jul 10
Lloyd's List: no large vessel transits on the Oman-hugging "Southern Highway" route since Jul 7, with at least two ships believed to have crossed dark (AIS off).¹
Jul 8-9
U.S. CENTCOM launches renewed strikes on Iranian military targets after three commercial tankers were hit near Hormuz; Iran claims retaliatory strikes on U.S.-linked sites in Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Jordan, Iraq.²

UN IMO Secretary-General condemns attacks on shipping and calls for "maximum restraint"; UN Secretary-General warns of catastrophic consequences if full-scale hostilities resume.²

EIA's July STEO (data cut-off Jul 1) still assumes the Jun 18 MOU holds — Brent forecast cut to $74/bbl for 3Q26; now overtaken by the Jul 7-8 escalation.⁹
Jun 19
Three Saudi VLCCs (~6 million bbl combined) transit the strait and reactivate AIS in the Gulf of Oman — the clearest test signal of post-MOU normalization before the relapse.¹⁵
Jun 18
